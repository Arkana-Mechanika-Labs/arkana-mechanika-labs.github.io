## 5.1 Miscellaneous Items

### Grappling Hook, Rope

{{< contributor "Alexander von Lünen" >}}
A rope is of use for leaving a town by the city wall, entering a cave on top of a mountain, getting yourself out of the pit in the mines and dragging a companion out of the quicksand when travelling through a bog. I believe a grappling hook supports most of these purposes as well. However, all occasions for using a rope include different options which will serve the special purpose more accurately. Leaving the pit with the help of a Stone-Tar potion is guaranteed success better than anything else, leaving the town by a sewer rather than climbing up the wall using a rope, and getting your comrade out of the mud is properly done with a Transformation potion.
{{< /contributor >}}

The game asks for a rope at the city wall, at the dwarf's chamber halfway up a cliff, when climbing into a castle keep at night and when building a raft in a flooded marsh. The Grappling Hook has no use at all: no encounter in the game looks for it. A rope is no help in the mine pit either. The ways out of the pit are climbing (the best Agility in the party, reduced by what he carries), a Stone-tar potion (always works), a Thunderbolt (it may work, and it hurts everyone), a human pyramid, digging (Artifice), a prayer, or waiting for the dwarves.

The peat bog has a "throw a rope" option, but because of an original bug it doesn't look for a rope. It is offered when someone carries a polearm, a bow or metal armour, and stays greyed out for a party that has a rope and none of those. The rope is never used up. The Transformation potion is the sure way: it saves the sinking member with everything he carries, and it isn't used up either.

### Harp, Flute

{{< contributor "C. Michel Boucher" >}}
To be used in combination with prayers to St. Cecilia, Patron Saint of music, who, among other things "enhances local reputation greatly if the person has a musical instrument".
{{< /contributor >}}

The character who prays must carry the harp or the flute, and the gain goes to the town you are in (see [3.9](../general/#39-getting-jobs)). Pray in a town: in the open country it is lost.

### Superb Horse, Fast Horse, Average Horse, Pack Horse, Mule

{{< contributor "Dennis Ahr" >}}
Superb horses increase the travel speed of the party. The best indicator for this is the night/day icon in the upper right corner of the display: it really slows down when the group has superb horses. It will speed up again in mountainous and forested areas or anywhere the going gets tough. I have never found whether the party needs only one superb horse or whether everyone needs one -- anyone know? I am not sure, but I think that the best places to seek out superb horses are in villages (that's where I usually find them) or monasteries.
{{< /contributor >}}

{{< contributor "Alexander von Lünen" >}}
With horses, you have the option to make an attempt to outrun boars, wolves and the Wild Hunt. Yet, I haven't figured out (or just can't remember) if this works if only one of your party members owns a horse. Nor did I find out if a fast or a superb horse increases your chance of success proportionally. But I guess your riding skill will affect this as well. Also, when meeting pilgrims, you can offer your horses and mules to them for easing their travellings, which may increase your virtue. What I can say, is that horses and so on are significant only if your purse grows too heavy }-) I haven't noticed a better traveling speed or anything like that.
{{< /contributor >}}

{{< contributor "C. Michel Boucher" >}}
Furthermore, horses appear in towns. They don't seem to affect the price of your lodgings, but they are mentioned if you go to the port at night, as part of an attempt to escape from a city.
{{< /contributor >}}

The answers to their questions:

- **Do horses make travel faster?** No. The time a step on the map takes depends only on the terrain, with or without horses (see [3.11](../general/#311-travelling-by-land)). The rules do show a horse bonus that was planned and never connected.
- **Where to buy them.** Only from the stablemaster at a city's inn. Villages and monasteries sell none. Whether a Superb Horse is on offer depends on the city's size, as for any rare item (see [3.3](../general/#where-do-i-find-the-ingredients)).
- **One horse or one each?** The option to outride wolves or boars, or a bishop's or a nobleman's guard, appears only when every member has a mount of his own. A mule counts. The Wild Hunt works differently (see [4.13](../quests/#413-wild-hunt)).
- **Does a better horse help?** Each horse has a quality: Superb 70, Fast 40, Average 25, Pack Horse 15, Mule 5. It counts against the Wild Hunt, in the race and joust with the knight on the road (see [4.5](../quests/#45-knight-enroute-to-jousting-tournament)) and when riding away from a guard on the road (see [4.6](../quests/#46-extortion-by-nobles-and-clergy)).
- **Wolves and boars.** Here horses barely help, because of two original bugs. Riding away from wolves depends only on the worst rider's Riding skill; the horse's quality is never added. Riding away from boars works two times in a hundred, whatever you ride.
- **Ships and lodging.** Lodging costs the same with or without horses. A passage by ship costs more per horse than per person (see [3.12](../general/#312-travelling-by-water)), and swimming away from a city at night costs you every horse.

### Tusk of a boar

{{< contributor "Dennis Ahr" >}}
I remember it being for good luck and only the leader needed it (one only). My recollection is that it was of only minor importance, but I can't recall what parameter you would check to see its effects. I surely don't remember any of the effects mentioned in the first email on this.
{{< /contributor >}}

DA's son Carey found another use for tusks which I had also noted when I was playing the game: Well, the only thing I remember the boar's tusk being useful for is using it as a "buffer" to protect your useful items when you're selling loot. Since stores won't buy a boar's tusk, the inventory menu will stop scrolling when it gets to the tusk, and any items above it in the menu won't be sold accidentally. Another good thing to use is clothing if your party is young and hasn't encountered boars.

The game gives the tusk to the leader after a fight with boars, and nothing ever looks at it again: it has no effect at all.

### Clock, Gold Cup, Wolfskin

- **Clock**: an ordinary item worth 1,000 pfennigs (just over four florins). It has no use, but it sells.
- **Gold Cup**: the dragon's golden cup, stolen from its hoard. While a dragon quest is running you may come upon the thief's trail. Holding the cup up to the dragon at its lair calms it, and the dragon remembers. It can't be sold.
- **Wolfskin**: nothing in the game uses it, and shops won't buy it.

## 5.2 Holy Relics

{{< contributor "C. Michel Boucher" >}}
These are found in evil monasteries, locked in chests. Once in the hands of the group, they are freed and can be returned to a cathedral (not just any church) in a city. The return of these holy relics does not appear to confer any direct benefits on your group in the way of improving individual characteristics, but each donation transfers 30 points or so to improve your local reputation. It would appear that a donation of more than one relic per day does not, however, grant more increases. If a player has more than one relic, he should spread the donations over a few days, or a few cities.

Some holy relics can be used by the group, others cannot. All those with 99q are not useable, no relic is saleable, and all weapons (with less than 99q) can be equipped and used as regular weapons. Other than their obvious quality, useable holy relics may or may not have other beneficial properties. Certainly, no items of such quality can be purchased anywhere.
{{< /contributor >}}

A donation does bring a direct benefit: it fills the whole party's Divine Favor to the top, every time. Catherine's Pain, listed among the unusable relics in earlier versions, is an 85q poniard you can fight with.

### Is there a limit of one relic a day?

No. Each donation is a separate try at 30 points of standing, and like every change of standing it is less likely to take the further your standing already is from zero (see [3.8](../general/#38-fame-and-reputation)). In a city that already loves you, most donations add nothing, which is easily mistaken for a daily limit. Standing is kept separately for each city, so spreading the relics still pays. The Divine Favor is restored every time.

### Where do relics come from?

The Templars' fortress monastery holds three of them, picked at random, in its chests. Because of an original bug, relics found there all come with quality 35 and as plain objects: a relic weapon from the fortress can't be wielded. The other source is a cathedral's canon. Sometimes he asks the party to deal with a raubritter near the city, and when that is done he gives you his cathedral's relic. Because of an original bug he never takes the reward back off his books: you can ask for the relic again and again, and donate each copy for standing and Divine Favor.

## 5.3 Unique Locales

{{< plate src="/images/faq/s5-devils-bridge.png" alt="A green, horned devil standing on an arched stone bridge over a stream, bare trees behind" side="right" pixel="true" >}}
A devil on a stone bridge. Scene picture from the game (MicroProse, 1992).
{{< /plate >}}

{{< contributor "C. Michel Boucher" >}}
The Devil's Bridge can be found before entering the village of South Nurn. The good witch can be found in a manor at Halberstadt, SW of Magdeburg. She will evaluate your level of training to determine whether you are capable of taking on the evil that lurks in the darklands. You can return to this place for a quick appraisal.
{{< /contributor >}}

The good witch is a magician-astrologer. Her visions only come once the witches' sabbats have started to trouble the land and the party is strong enough by the game's own measure, the "lore level" that also sets the size of the bands you fight (see [3.13](../general/#313-fighting-tactics)). Below level 3 she sends you away with nothing. Otherwise she tells you the next step against the witches and the Templars, and a party that is still on the weak side may get a Transformation potion from her.

At the Devil's Bridge you can cross, and meet him, or take six hours to go round.

## 5.5 Cathedrals and Universities

{{< contributor "C. Michel Boucher" >}}
In Universities, you can combine most of the functions of some of the other learning institutions, the Kloster and the Alchemist. The University will allow you to learn about saints or purchase alchemical material (the physician also), and to upgrade your Philosopher's Stone. Furthermore, you can hire the services of a professor to teach the following subjects: Alchemy, Religion, Speak Latin, Read and Write and Healing. The first can also be obtained from an Alchemist, the middle three from a Kloster and the last from a Physician. Note that cities not listed here have neither a Cathedral nor a University.
{{< /contributor >}}

**Towns with universities (13):** Basel, Erfurt, Freiberg, Freiberg-im-Breisgau, Heidelberg, Köln, Leipzig, Nürnberg, Prag, Pressburg, Rostock, Wien, Würzburg.

The game spells both Freibergs "Freiberg": the one in the Breisgau (Freiburg im Breisgau today) is "Freiberg-im-Breisgau". Wittenberg has a name for a university in its record, but no university.

## 5.6 Random Tips

As was pointed out by Chris Meadows, clothiers will never sell you anything during the day, but if you go back at night... you can wake somebody to trade. The chance is the leader's Charisma plus half his Speak Common plus your standing in the town, at most 90%, and the town likes you a little less each time you try.

Dennis Ahr tossed some random goodness our way:

{{< contributor "Dennis Ahr" >}}
The person using a rope to climb up something should remove all armor and weapons before climbing.

If the party is fighting just one strong enemy (raubritter, demon, etc.) he will fight with only one of your party. That means the other three may do battle using their berserk mode thus making short work of the enemy.

Once the party has some experience and skill, they may gain money at an accelerated pace by scouring the countryside for castles with evil rulers. Using St. Gabriel, St. Hildegard or St. Isidore, the party can discern whether or not the ruler is a good man or an evil man and then sneak into the castle if he is evil. There are usually bountiful goodies inside.

A note on the Wild Hunt. I think someone mentioned some specific saints who would stop the Wild Hunt. In my experience, the saint needed to end the hunt is never the same from one game to the next.
{{< /contributor >}}

Climbing, like sneaking and dodging, uses Agility as the load allows. Up to half of Strength plus Endurance a character climbs with his full Agility; up to the whole of it with two thirds; up to one and a half times with a third; beyond that with 1.

A castle lord's nature is fixed for the whole game: the castle you scouted stays good or evil. Asking the farmers nearby also tells you.

{{< contributor "C. Michel Boucher" >}}
The saint even changes within a single game, once you have defeated the hunt with one saint. If the hunt is called on you again, the Holzfrau will tell you the name of another saint. I presume it is the name of a saint you do not know at the time.
{{< /contributor >}}

{{< contributor "Myles Martinez" >}}
Actually, it IS possible to already have knowledge of the proper saint to dispel the Hunt on its first attack.
{{< /contributor >}}

Myles is right: each new Hunt draws its saint at random from all 136, so you may know it already, and the option to call on him appears whether or not the Holzfrau has told you (see [4.13](../quests/#413-wild-hunt)).

### More tips

- **Bank your money before a night walk.** The night watch's fine grows with every florin in your purse (see [3.6](../general/#36-cities-and-villages)).
- **Never accept the forest witch's "unnatural strength".** It kills your leader on the spot (see [4.10](../quests/#410-solitary-women)).
- **Don't try to pick the lock of the dwarves' stone box in the mines.** It always fails (see [4.9](../quests/#49-mines-and-knockers)).
- **Don't drink Essence o'Grace while Ironarm is working.** If Ironarm (or a saint) has lifted Strength above its maximum, the Essence pulls it back down (see [3.3](../general/#33-alchemy)).

## 5.7 Stupid Things About Darklands

C. Michel Boucher has noted some things that sequel makers should remedy in future incarnations:

{{< contributor "C. Michel Boucher" >}}
Characters have no real limit on how much they can carry, even though weight for each object is given.

There is never a shortage of any particular good for sale. No matter where you go, you can always buy any number of a particular item for sale. Considering that Germany was not yet the rich nation it later became, it seems unlikely there would be an unending number of all items for sale at any given time.

There is no difference in size for various characters (Gretch can wear Gunther's armour and vice versa, an unlikely occurence in real life).

There is no need to eat or sleep while on the road. Normally, I would say this has been abstracted for the sake of simplicity, but for a game that actually requires you to decide what your characters do every hour of every day, you would think eating and sleeping might be requirements, rather than simply optional. Also, travelling is continuous, with no stops at night, unless you desire to do so. Your characters will cross Germany from end to end without stopping to rest or eat.

Travelling further by water does not cost more. Assuming it costs 3 groschen to go from Vordingbord to Naskskov, and it costs 3 groschen to go from Naskskov to Flensburg, it doesn't cost more than one fare to go from Vordingbord to Naskskov and continue on to Flensburg and even further, all the way to Thorn, if the opportunity arises.

Germans are extremely honest people; nothing you carry with you is ever stolen without your knowledge, or being able to fight to keep it.

You are never caught unaware, with your pants down and your weapons unready, unless the player has chosen to unequip his characters ahead of time.

No one ever bolts from combat, everyone fights to the death, unless the player chooses otherwise. Non-player characters die with their boots (or hooves) on.

A player character who has not been attacked will not involve itself in the defense of others, but a non-player character will.

Characters on the point of death can still strike with full force.

Armour and weapons never degrade as a result of regular combat. Only alchemical attacks cause armour to degrade.

Characters can pass things to one another, even though they're rooms apart.

The Archbishop of Trier has two capitals, Trier and Koblenz.
{{< /contributor >}}

Some notes from the game's rules:

- **Carrying.** There is no limit on weight, but the load counts: the more a character carries compared with his Strength and Endurance, the less of his Agility he can use, down to a single point (see [5.6](#56-random-tips)).
- **Shops.** A shop never runs out of what it stocks. But not every shop stocks everything: rare goods are on offer far more often in large cities, and each shop's choice is fixed for the whole game.
- **Theft.** It does happen without a fight: a member sinking in a bog loses part of his belongings, swimming away from a city at night costs all horses and armour and much of the rest, and a stay in a city's dungeon costs your money and most of what you carry.
- **Ships.** Right: the fare covers the first leg only, and sailing on from the wharf where you arrive is free.
- **Wear.** Right as well, with one bug: when alchemy wears down a piece of armour, every identical spare piece in the same stack wears down with it.
