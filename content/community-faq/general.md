## 3.3 Alchemy

{{< plate src="/images/faq/alchemist-laboratory.png" alt="An alchemist's laboratory: flasks on a shelf, a hanging lamp, a table with vessels" side="right" pixel="true" >}}
An alchemist's laboratory. Scene picture from the game (MicroProse, 1992).
{{< /plate >}}

One of the many aspects of Darklands that sets it apart from cookie-cutter D&D games is its use of Alchemy, as opposed to magic. With alchemical formulae and their corresponding ingredients, characters can create a variety of potions. The formulae must be learned and the ingredients procured, however. They don't just fall into your lap. Based upon your character's Intelligence and Alchemy skill, the quality of your philosopher's stone, and some good old-fashioned luck, you'll be creating potions in no time. Options to create potions appear when the party has set up camp or taken residence in a city/village inn. From there the character can choose to concoct potions that he has learned.

{{< contributor "Alexander von Lünen" >}}
First of all, get your Alchemist the best training he or she can get. Training is provided by alchemists in town and at universities. Next thing, get formulas and ingredients. Alchemists in town will trade formulas, and some sell a few. Getting ingredients can be a pain as some stuff is rather rare. Be sure to buy a lot of a rare item once you have found it. You'll need a lot of all the kinds of bases, above all Sanguine and Choleric Base, which go into more formulas than anything else. Very important are Aqua Regia (for Thunderbolt potions) and Camomile (for two of the three Essence o'Grace formulas), and both are darned hard to find. Below is a list of potions which are in my opinion very useful:

- **Essence o'Grace** - A must! Assure yourself of having about 20 potions at hand during normal travelling. For special tasks, you have to figure out the number according to your characters' vulnerability at that special time. I took over 80 potions to the Great Monastery with me and even ran low!
- **Thunderbolt** - Necessary as well. Needed as a throwing weapon in combat, useful when cracking locks and to get "access" to a castle. All-round potion.
- **Transformation** - Very helpful. Turns an evil altar into white marble, and can get a character out of the quicksand really quickly. Needs pure gold to be made.
- **Stone-Tar** - Should be in the inventory of every good alchemist. Helps you getting out of a pit, slows your enemies to provide you with accurate time to deal with them.
- **Firewall** - Of great use when dealing with dragons and evil alchemists.

Everything else is depending on one's preference. Arabian Fire and Breath Of Death might be a good choice in combat, too.

Buy ready-made potions only if you haven't got the formula of that potion and you desperately need it. Potions bought in cities have a quality of 25q, while self-made can have 35q-45q, if you brew them from the second or third formula of the potion! In case of Essence o' Grace, this can make a great difference in restoring strength. A nice occasion for getting new formulas is the witch in the forest. After defeating her, she offers you three formulas for her life (among other options).
{{< /contributor >}}

### How good will my potions be?

{{< plate src="/images/faq/brunschwig.jpg" alt="Woodcut of two men distilling with furnaces and alembics" side="right" size="small" >}}
Distilling, from Hieronymus Brunschwig's *Liber de arte distillandi* (Strassburg, 1512). Science History Institute, via Wikimedia Commons, public domain.
{{< /plate >}}

Each potion has three formulas, named after different alchemists (Galen's, Michael Scot's and Avicenna's Essence o'Grace, for example). A potion brewed from the first formula is quality 25, from the second 35, from the third 45. The later formulas are harder to brew, but they often need fewer ingredients: Avicenna's Essence o'Grace takes five units of ingredients against Galen's nine. Potions bought from an alchemist are always 25.

Quality matters most for Essence o'Grace. It gives back twice its quality in percent of the Strength the drinker has lost: a 25q potion restores half, a 45q potion nine tenths. It also raises Endurance for about 34 hours, by 9 to 14 points at 25q and up to 22 to 29 at 45q. Don't drink it while an Ironarm potion or a saint is holding your Strength above its maximum: the Essence pulls it back down.

### What are my chances of brewing a potion?

<p class="dl-formula">Chance (%) = 100 + Intelligence + Alchemy + philosopher's stone − the formula's difficulty<br><small>at most 99, for the alchemist who brews</small></p>

The difficulty (the formula's "mystic number") runs from 80 for al-Razi's Noxious Aroma to 210 for Nicolaus of A's Transformation; the Essence o'Grace formulas are among the easiest, 81 to 98. All potions of one formula brewed at the same time succeed or fail together. Up to three at once, the chance is as above; for more, the 100 is divided: 50 for four or five, 33 for six or seven, 25 for eight or nine. So a less skilled alchemist should brew in batches of three.

A failed brew loses the ingredients. In a town or village, half the time it also causes an accident, and the inn or lodging will not have the party back for 30 days, or 120 days if the brew exploded. Brewing in camp in the wilderness carries no such risk. The game rates formulas as low, medium and high risk, but because of an original bug it works backwards: low-risk formulas explode in three failures out of four, high-risk ones in half.

Success and failure both train the Alchemy skill. Brews finish the next time time passes; if you leave the camp first, the brew is lost.

### Why can't I buy Essence o'Grace, Stone-tar or Transformation formulas?

{{< plate src="/images/faq/city-alchemist.png" alt="An old alchemist writing at his desk among flasks" side="right" pixel="true" >}}
An alchemist at his desk. Scene picture from the game (MicroProse, 1992).
{{< /plate >}}

Because of an original bug, the alchemists who sell formulas only ever offer the first 22 of the 66: the three formulas of Noxious Aroma, Eyeburn, Black Cloud, Fleadust, Eater Water, Breath of Death and Sunburst, and al-Tamimi's Thunderbolt. Each town offers four of them, for 10, 20 or 30 florins (first, second or third formula) divided by the town's size. Every other formula has to come from elsewhere:

- **The formula exchange.** A town's best alchemist sees the party only by day, and only if your best alchemist's skill impresses him (Alchemy 30 or better always does). Despite the name he takes nothing in exchange. In four towns out of five he wants your best Alchemy skill above a figure between 30 and 79, which is fixed for each town. If he agrees, he teaches the party's leader one formula at random, usually of a potion the leader doesn't yet know. So make your alchemist the leader before you ask.
- **The forest witch.** Beaten and at your mercy, she offers three formulas for her life. They are three random picks given to each of the first four party members, and because of an original bug most picks miss: they land on another member, or teach a saint instead. **Never take her "unnatural strength"**: it kills your leader on the spot.
- Your starting characters' formulas, and rewards from some quests.

### Where do I find the ingredients?

Herbalists' stalls in market squares, university shops, physicians, the shops of mining villages, and the alchemists who don't sell potions sell them. The four bases are common everywhere, and Brimstone and Pure Gold nearly so. Nikel, Solanaceae, Aqua Regia, Camomile, Gum, Mondragora, Alum, Zinken and Pitchblende are rare; Zincblende and Antimoni rarer; Orpiment, White Cinnabar and Eastern Black Bean rarer still; Manganes and Naptha very rare; and Marsh Vapor, needed for Breath of Death, the rarest of all. Big cities stock rare goods far more often than small towns.

A shop's stock is fixed for the whole game: if a town has no Camomile today, it never will, so don't wait for it. Go to another town.

Ingredients don't need to be carried by the alchemist. A brew takes what it needs from the whole party, the alchemist's own packs first.

### What does the philosopher's stone do?

Its quality, from 1 to 25, adds straight to the chance of every brew, and it belongs to the party, not to one character. A town's best alchemist sells a better one if his is better than yours. His stone's quality is fixed for each town, so it pays to ask in several towns.

### Using the weapon and armour potions

Use them from the character sheet (press P on the potion in the inventory). The effect goes to the character, not to a particular weapon or piece of armour. Because of original bugs, several last much longer than the manual says:

- **Deadly Blade, Strongedge and Great Power** never wear off, and further doses add up. After three or four doses the counter overflows, and the bonus can fall back to almost nothing. Great Power adds to damage and penetration rather than to the weapon's quality.
- **Hardarmor** never wears off either. It adds one to three levels of armour thickness (by quality) and no quality.
- **Firewall** gives fire resistance of 50, 70 or 90 (by quality) and never wears off. Through the same bug it also counts as extra armour.
- **Trueflight, Truesight, New-wind, Ironarm and Quickmove** last about a day, as the manual says.

Drinking Transformation does nothing but add one florin to your purse: it is meant to be used in particular places, such as an evil altar or the quicksand of a peat bog.
