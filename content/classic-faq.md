The [Darklands FAQ v3.0](http://www.darklands.net/faq/faq.shtml) by C. Michel Boucher, Fred Farzanegan, Dennis Ahr and Alexander von Lünen, edited by Myles Q. Martinez, has been the players' guide to Darklands since 1996. Its authors worked from play, the manual and the hint book. The restoration has the rules of the original program itself, so the FAQ's claims can now be checked against them.

This page does not reproduce the FAQ: read it on [darklands.net](http://www.darklands.net/faq/faq.shtml), and keep this page beside it. Each entry names the FAQ section, says briefly what the FAQ claims, and gives what the game does. About 250 claims were checked. Most of the FAQ is right; the entries here are the ones where the game differs, or where the rule turns out to be more precise than play could show.

- **Wrong**: the game does something else.
- **More precisely**: right in spirit; here is the exact rule.
- **Not in the game**: no rule of the program does this.

All figures are those of version 483.07. Where a figure comes from an original bug, the [Bugs & Oddities](/formats/original-bugs-and-oddities/) page lists it.

## 2 Technical assistance

### 2.1 Saving in battle

**More precisely.** The FAQ says only some large battlefields allow saving, when no enemy is left on the floor. The rule is: Save Game is offered on a battlefield the game keeps between visits, when the party is not in real combat (no enemy has noticed you yet, or the last one is down). The mines, the Templar fortress, tombs and crypts, and Baphomet's citadel qualify. Castle and keep fights never do.

### 2.1 Loading from inside the game

**More precisely.** You can still answer No to *Loading is irreversable. Proceed?* After Yes, the party and the event log are already cleared before the load screen opens, so pressing Esc there carries on with an empty party. Pick a saved game instead. This is an original bug.

### 2.1 No specific ending

**Wrong.** There is an ending. Refusing Baphomet's seventh offer at the Gate of the Six Keys plays the ending film and gives 200 fame (133 on Basic, 300 on Expert). The party then wakes at the nearest city's inn and the game goes on.

### 2.1 Fame

**More precisely.** The Party Information screen names your fame in steps of 50, from *Unknown* up to *Legendary Heroes* at 500 or more.

### 2.1 "Freiburg B"

**Wrong.** The game never writes "Freiburg". Both towns are "Freiberg" in the game's own data, the one in the Breisgau with the full name "Freiberg-im-Breisgau". Only the lord's title, Count of Freiburg, has the right spelling.

## 3 General game considerations

### 3.3 Potion quality

**More precisely.** Bought potions are quality 25. A potion you brew is 25, 35 or 45, according to which of its three formulas you used: the second formula gives 35, the third 45. The later formulas are harder to brew.

### 3.3 Buying formulas

**More precisely.** Because of an original bug, city alchemists only ever offer the first 22 formulas, so formulas such as Essence o'Grace are never for sale. They can still be learned elsewhere: the alchemist's formula exchange, a City Lord's reward, the forest witch, the Templars' library.

### 3.4 St. Giles of Portugal

**Wrong.** The FAQ says he costs Strength and Endurance for good. A successful prayer costs the praying character 1 point of current Strength, which rest restores, and 1 point of Virtue. In return he gives Alchemy +40 to 59, Artifice +10 to 19, Intelligence +5 to 9 and Perception +15 to 24 for a day: a saint for alchemists.

### 3.4 The saints' texts

**More precisely.** The saints' descriptions in the game are only a rough guide. For several dozen saints the effect differs from the text: St. Giles swaps the Streetwise and Woodwise boosts, St. George improves no equipment, St. Hedwig heals nothing, and so on.

### 3.5 Chainmail

**Wrong.** Chainmail is not light. It weighs 23 on the body and 26 on the limbs, against 28 and 31 for plate; Cuirbouilli, at 6 and 8, is the light armour. Your burden limit is Strength + Endurance, counting only what is worn and wielded: up to half the limit Agility is full, up to the limit it is two thirds, up to one and a half times the limit one third, and beyond that 1.

### 3.7 The night watch

**More precisely.** The watch's fine per party member is 1 pfennig + the city's size + one pfennig for each florin in your purse, less a little at high standing. Bank or spend your florins before walking the streets at night. Fighting the watch costs 15 to 25 standing and makes the city hostile for 5 days, or 10 if your standing has fallen to −75 or below. It is not for ever.

### 3.9 Town leaders do pay

**Wrong.** The FAQ says town leaders pay nothing (Jerry Hagen's correction is right). The council hall pays the city's size × 7 florins for a commission and size × 3 for an errand. A fortress commission pays no money.

### 3.9 St. Cecilia

**More precisely.** Cecilia raises your standing only in the town you are in, by 20 to 59 points (with the usual chance, in percent, of 100 minus your standing there, whatever its sign), and only if the praying character carries a harp or a flute. In the open country she does nothing.

### 3.10 Hired companions

**Wrong.** Neither stays a year. The Hanseatic knight stays 360 days and the village Schulz 90 days. Neither leaves when the quest ends, but the Hanse knight is dismissed when the Hanse pays you for an errand.

### 3.13 Ganging up

**More precisely.** Each extra fighter in melee with the same enemy adds 10 to everyone's chance to hit him, and each fighter on him takes 10 off his own chance to hit. Concentrate your attacks.

### 3.14 Enemy weapons

**Wrong.** In the enemy table the skeleton's battle axe is quality 20, not 10, and the dwarf's maul 25, not 15. The dwarf overlord's maul is 55.

### 3.14 Citadel, room 6

**Wrong.** The lancers cost Strength and Endurance only, not a quarter of every attribute. Single combat costs the champion half of his Strength and Endurance maximum if he wins and a quarter if he loses; a whole-party attack costs everyone a quarter on a win and a sixth on a loss; giving blood costs everyone 5 points of maximum Strength.

### 3.15 Age

**Not in the game.** Age only matters while the character is created. In play a character ages a year at a time, and no rule lowers attributes or skills with age: there is no reason to retire at 40.

### 3.15 Retiring

**More precisely.** Retiring a companion at the inn costs the party one fifth of its purse and one fifth of its bank notes; the money is not kept anywhere. Hired companions leave without taking anything.

## 4 Specific quests and encounters

### 4.3 The friendly alchemist

**Wrong.** His trade takes your potion and gives you a cheaper one, and what he asks for never overlaps what he offers, so the trade cannot be repeated.

### 4.5 The knight on his way to the tournament

**Wrong.** Beating him gives no fame and no standing. A race win trains Riding; either win tops your purse up to 1 florin if you have less.

### 4.6 The Dominican

**More precisely.** Refusing him does not curse you by itself. The curse falls if you walk away while he chants, if a prayer against it fails, or if you lose the fight. It hits every member: each loses (100 − Virtue) / 14 + 1 Strength and Endurance, half of that from the maximum, and Divine Favour.

### 4.6 Bishops and noblemen

**More precisely.** Fighting a bishop's guard costs every member 2 Virtue and 25 Divine Favour, whatever the outcome; fighting a nobleman's guard costs no Virtue. On a win, the nearest city's standing drops by 10 to 19 for the bishop and 20 to 29 for the nobleman.

### 4.13 The Wild Hunt

**Wrong.** It can be stopped. Once a member knows the saint the Holzfrau names, a line lets you call on that saint, which ends the Hunt without a roll. It is not gone for good: a later event can raise it again, with a new saint.

### 4.14 Victory at the High Sabbat

**Wrong.** Every member gains 3 Virtue, not 1 or 2. Divine Favour is restored to the maximum, the party gains 25 fame, and the nearest city's standing rises by 25 to 49.

### 4.16 Entering Baphomet's castle

**Wrong.** You do not need both seals. The gate checks for the first seal broken, and either the High Sabbat victory or the demon prince's scene in the Templar monastery breaks it.

## 5 Miscellaneous tidbits

### 5.1 Horses

**Wrong.** Horses do not make you travel faster: time on the map depends on the terrain alone. The original works out whether the whole party is mounted and never uses the result, so the speed bonus the hint book describes was never connected. Horses are bought from the stablemaster at a city inn.

### 5.1 Outrunning on horseback

**More precisely.** The "ride away" lines appear only when every member present is fit and has a mount; a mule counts. Against boars the chance shown is always 1%, and the real one about 2%. Against wolves it is the worst Riding skill in the party. The horse's quality never counts: the original reads the wrong item flags.

### 5.1 A rope in the peat bog

**More precisely.** Throwing a rope to a sinking companion pulls with the party's average Agility plus three times its average Strength, less what the victim carries; pulling by hand counts Strength twice. But the line is not unlocked by a rope. Because of an original bug, it is offered when someone carries a polearm, a bow or metal armour, and a rope alone does not unlock it.

### 5.1 Rope, grappling hook and boar's tusk

**Not in the game.** The pit in the mines has no rope option. No card ever uses the grappling hook: the city walls, cliffs and rafts all ask for the Rope. The boar's tusk is given to the leader and read by nothing.

### 5.2 Relics

**More precisely.** There is no limit of one donation a day. Each relic donated at a cathedral is its own roll, with a chance, in percent, of 101 minus your standing there (whatever its sign) of raising that standing by 30. Each donation also restores every member's Divine Favour to the maximum. Catherine's Pain is a usable poniard of quality 85. No relic can be sold.

### 5.5 Cathedrals and universities

**Wrong.** The lists are incomplete. 48 cities have a cathedral: Aachen, Augsburg, Bamberg, Basel, Brandenburg, Braunschweig, Breslau, Bremen, Brünn, Danzig, Dresden, Erfurt, Flensburg, Frankfurt am Main, Frankfurt an der Oder, both Freibergs, Goslar, Graz, Hamburg, Köln, Konstanz, Leipzig, Lübeck, Luxemburg, Magdeburg, Mainz, München, Münster, Osnabrück, Paderborn, Prag, Pressburg, Regensburg, Salzburg, Schleswig, Soest, Speyer, Strassburg, Stuttgart, Thorn, Trier, Ulm, Wien, Worms, Würzburg, Xanten and Zürich. 13 have a university: Basel, Erfurt, both Freibergs, Heidelberg, Köln, Leipzig, Nürnberg, Prag, Pressburg, Rostock, Wien and Würzburg. Wittenberg names a university but has none.

### 5.6 A castle lord's nature

**Wrong.** St. Roch and St. Reinold do not reveal it. At the castle on the knoll, St. Gabriel, St. Hildegard or St. Isidore gives a vision of the lord. Whether a lord is cruel or just is fixed for the whole game.

## 6 Summary of the hint book

### Wealthy urban childhood

**Wrong.** It gives Perception +2, Intelligence +1 and Charisma +1, and no change to Endurance. The experience and skill figures in the hint book's table are right.

### The Preceptor's sword

**Wrong.** The Templar fights with a two-handed sword of quality 99 and leaves one of quality 55, not 45.

### St. Peter

**Wrong.** His minimum Virtue is 58, not 53. The 25 to 75 Divine Favour and the base chance of 55 are right.
